    Method. Each measure scores 0–10 on a straight line between a "0 points" and a "10 points" value (hover a measure to see them), adjusted for the company's industry, taken from its SEC industry code: a 20% gross margin is poor for a software company and normal for a retailer. Pillars average their measures and are weighted: growth 20%, profitability 25%, balance sheet 15%, dilution 10%, valuation 20%, momentum 10%. Banks and insurers are scored on measures that suit them (return on equity, capital, price to book). Grades: A 8+, B 6.5+, C 5+, D 3.5+, F below. The colour at the top of the page follows the score. The thresholds and weights are judgement calls and have not been back-tested against share-price returns.

    Sources. The company's financial statements and industry code from SEC EDGAR, read when you ask; the share price from Yahoo Finance, Nasdaq or Stooq, whichever answers first. Scores are kept for up to an hour, so repeat visits are instant.

    Independent project, not affiliated with or endorsed by any company it scores. A structured summary of public numbers, not a forecast, a recommendation or investment advice. See also the in-depth Opendoor Scorecard.
